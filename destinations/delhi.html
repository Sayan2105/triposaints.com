Delhi

Seven cities on the same patch of ground

Evening in Lodhi Garden

01Story

A capital that keeps burying its previous versions

Delhi has been destroyed and rebuilt so often that historians settled on a number — seven cities — and then kept finding more. You can drive from a twelfth-century minaret in Mehrauli to a 1930s viceroy's avenue in twenty minutes, past tombs that people use as shortcuts and cricket pitches. The count starts before the historians do: tradition places Indraprastha, the Pandavas' capital in the Mahabharata, under the mound where Purana Qila now stands — a village called Indrapat lived on inside its walls until it was cleared in 1913.

Almost nobody likes Delhi on the first day. It is loud, enormous and entirely indifferent to you. Give it three days and it starts handing things over: a courtyard, a meal, directions from a stranger delivered as a ten-minute monologue.

02Culture

The city argues about dinner

Delhi argues about food the way other cities argue about football. Old Delhi does kebabs and korma in the lanes around Jama Masjid; Punjabi families who arrived after Partition in 1947 gave the city butter chicken and its whole idea of a good evening.

On Thursday evenings qawwali is sung at Nizamuddin Dargah as it has been for centuries — sit at the back, cover your head, and stay past the first song. The Sunday book market on Daryaganj and the Lodhi Garden tombs at dusk are the city simply being itself, with no ticket involved.

03Practical guidance

The honest logistics

  • Best monthsOctober to March. Air quality is at its worst from November into January — plan around it if you have asthma.
  • Getting thereIndira Gandhi International (DEL) is India's main gateway; the Airport Express metro reaches New Delhi station in about twenty minutes.
  • StayingNizamuddin East or Lodhi Road for gardens and quiet; Hauz Khas for cafés; Paharganj only if budget decides everything.
  • Days neededThree full days covers Old Delhi, the Mehrauli–Qutb belt and Lutyens' Delhi without rushing.
  • Budget feelFrom about ₹2,000/day on a budget; ₹6,000/day buys real comfort and a car with a driver.
04Local insight

What a Saint will tell you

Use the metro — clean, cheap, and faster than any car in Delhi traffic — and take a cycle rickshaw only inside Old Delhi, where nothing wider fits. Eat breakfast at a halwai in Chandni Chowk or Karol Bagh, not at the hotel buffet. Sunder Nursery, next door to Humayun's Tomb, is the best hour in the city and most visitors skip it. In Mehrauli, spare twenty minutes for the Yogmaya Temple a few lanes from the Qutb — dedicated to Krishna's sister and by most reckonings one of Delhi's oldest living shrines, still in daily worship while the minaret takes the photographs. Fix fares before starting, or use an app.

05Responsible travel

Being a good guest

Delhi's monuments double as neighbourhood parks — dress as though you are in someone's locality, because you are. Decline the 'government emporium' detour a driver offers; it is a commission racket, not a shop. Refill your bottle instead of buying six a day.
