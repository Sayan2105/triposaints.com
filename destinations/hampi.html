Karnataka · The Deccan

Hampi

An empire's capital, left out in the open

The Vijayanagara ruins above the Tungabhadra

01Story

A city that was never rebuilt

For roughly two centuries Vijayanagara was one of the largest cities on Earth. Persian and Portuguese visitors wrote home about its bazaars, its irrigation and its wealth. In 1565 a coalition of Deccan sultanates broke its army at Talikota, and the city was wrecked methodically over the months that followed.

The strange part is that nobody buried it. Some 40 square kilometres of granite boulders above the Tungabhadra still hold temples, aqueducts, stables and market streets standing in daylight where they were abandoned. Farmers grow bananas between them.

02Culture

Ruins that are still in use

Hampi is not purely archaeology. The Virupaksha temple at the west end has never stopped functioning — worship in the same courtyard has run for centuries, and there is usually a wedding party in it. The Vittala complex further east holds the stone chariot printed on the ₹50 note, and pillars that ring when struck, which is why they are roped off now.

Across the river is Anegundi, older than the city it fathered, and the country the Ramayana calls Kishkindha. Anjanadri hill above it is held to be Hanuman's birthplace; it is several hundred steps and most people climb it before the sun does.

03Practical guidance

The honest logistics

  • Best monthsNovember to February. March to May routinely passes 40°C and the rock throws it back at you.
  • Getting thereHosapete (Hospet) station is 13 km away, with overnight trains from Bengaluru and Goa. Otherwise fly to Hubballi (HBX) or Bengaluru (BLR) and drive.
  • StayingHotels in Hosapete and Kamalapura; quieter guesthouses on the north bank across the river — check current rules before you book.
  • Days neededThree full days. One is a forced march; two misses the north bank entirely.
  • Budget feelGuesthouse and local food from ₹1,500/day; hotel comfort with a car and guide from ₹6,000/day.
04Local insight

What a Saint will tell you

Climb Matanga hill for sunrise and you will understand the layout of the whole city before breakfast. Take an ASI-approved guide through the royal enclosure — without one it is a field of foundations. Cross to the north bank by the small boat, rent a cycle, and give an afternoon to the banana fields. Carry two litres of water; there is no shade between monuments.

05Responsible travel

Being a good guest

These are protected monuments and, in places, working temples. Don't climb on carvings for photographs, don't take rubbings, and leave loose stone where it lies. Buy your lunch and water in the village rather than carrying in packaged food you will only have to leave behind.

06Next journey

Where this path continues

Hyderabad is the honest sequel — Golconda, seat of one of the sultanates in that coalition, still stands on its western edge and you can walk through it. Badami's cave temples are the other direction, if you want more carved rock.
