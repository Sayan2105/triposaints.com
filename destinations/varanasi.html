Varanasi

The city older than memory

Dashashwamedh Ghat at dawn

01Story

A city that outlived its own history

Mark Twain wrote that Varanasi is older than history, older than tradition, older even than legend. Stand on the ghats before sunrise and the line stops being a quotation. Bells begin somewhere uphill. Boats knock softly against stone steps that have been worn by feet for longer than most nations have existed.

People come here to pray, to mourn, to marry, to die well. The city does not perform its rituals for visitors; it simply continues them, and lets you watch. That is its gift — the feeling of standing inside continuity.

02Culture

The river is the culture

Everything faces the Ganges. Mornings belong to bathers and priests, evenings to the Ganga Aarti — a fire ceremony performed nightly for centuries, equal parts devotion and choreography. The old city behind the ghats is a maze of silk weavers, wrestling akharas, chai stalls and the finest breakfast kachori you will ever eat.

Music matters here: this is the city of the tabla and of Ravi Shankar. Ask your Saint about a baithak — a small private concert in an old haveli — and you'll understand why nights in Varanasi are as sacred as its mornings.

03Practical guidance

The honest logistics

  • Best monthsOctober to March — cool mornings, luminous light. Avoid May–June heat.
  • Getting thereFly to LKO/VNS or take the overnight train from Delhi — arriving by rail at dawn is its own experience.
  • StayingGhat-side heritage stays for atmosphere; Assi Ghat area for calmer nights.
  • Days neededTwo full days minimum; three lets the city slow you down properly.
  • Budget feelComfortable from ₹2,500/day; heritage comfort from ₹6,000/day.
04Local insight

What a Saint will tell you

Skip the crowded main aarti once — watch it instead from a rowing boat on the water, lamps doubling in the river. Breakfast is malaiyyo in winter, eaten standing up. The lanes are not a detour from the ghats; they are the other half of the city. And the burning ghats deserve reverence, not photography — your Saint will explain what is welcome and what is not.

05Responsible travel

Being a good guest

Varanasi is a living place of worship, not a set. Dress modestly on the ghats, ask before photographing people, and never photograph cremations. Choose the river's rowing boats over motor launches when you can — quieter for you, kinder to the river everyone here calls Mother.

06Next journey

Where this path continues

Travellers moved by Varanasi tend to love Bodh Gaya — two hours by road, and the place where the Buddha found what he was looking for. Or continue upriver to Rishikesh, where the same Ganges is young, cold and fast.
