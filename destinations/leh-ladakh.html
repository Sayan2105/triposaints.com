Ladakh · The Indian Himalaya

Leh–Ladakh

Where the air teaches patience

The high road through Ladakh

01Story

A high desert with a gentle heart

Ladakh is what remains when everything unnecessary is removed: rock, light, sky, and the people who learned to live beautifully between them. At 3,500 metres the air is thin enough to slow your steps — which turns out to be the point. Nothing here rewards hurry.

Prayer flags don't decorate this landscape; they translate it. Every pass, every rooftop sends something upward. You come for the mountains and stay changed by the calm.

02Culture

Monastery time

Ladakh runs on monastery time — drums at dawn from Thiksey, butter-lamp light in Hemis, novice monks racing each other down whitewashed steps. Buddhism here is not a museum piece; it is the neighbourhood.

In Leh's old town, sit for gur-gur chai — salted butter tea that makes sudden sense at altitude — and listen. Ladakhi hospitality is quiet and total.

03Practical guidance

The honest logistics

  • Best monthsLate May to September. Winter is possible, profound and seriously cold.
  • Getting thereFly into Leh (IXL) — then do nothing for 36–48 hours. Acclimatisation is not optional.
  • AltitudeLeh sits at 3,500 m; passes exceed 5,000 m. Hydrate, move slowly, take it seriously.
  • Days neededSix days minimum with acclimatisation; nine lets you reach Pangong and Nubra without racing.
  • PermitsInner Line Permits are needed for Pangong, Nubra and Tso Moriri — your Saint arranges them in a day.
04Local insight

What a Saint will tell you

Sunrise at Thiksey beats any viewpoint — arrive for the morning prayers, not after them. Pangong is glorious, but locals love Tso Moriri more: further, quieter, bluer. Eat where the drivers eat in Leh market. And buy apricots — Ladakh's are the best on Earth, and every purchase stays in the valley.

05Responsible travel

Being a good guest

This is one of the most fragile places you will ever visit. Water is precious — choose homestays that use traditional dry toilets over water-hungry hotels. Carry your plastic out. Ask before photographing monks or ceremonies. Your presence funds this valley; make sure it also respects it.

06Next journey

Where this path continues

After Ladakh's silence, travellers often want its opposite — the living noise of Varanasi — or the green Himalaya of Himachal, where the same mountains grow forests.
