Andaman & Nicobar Islands · Bay of Bengal

Andaman Islands — Port Blair & Havelock

A prison colony that became a sea

Radhanagar Beach, Havelock

01Story

The islands India sent people to forget

The Andamans sit closer to Myanmar than to the Indian mainland, and they run on their own logic — tides, ferry timetables, and a sun that rises before five because the whole country keeps Delhi's clock. What looks like a straightforward beach holiday has a harder history underneath it.

Port Blair's Cellular Jail was built to hold Indian freedom fighters as far from home as an empire could manage, and its corridors are still the quietest place in town. Sail out to Havelock the next morning and the same sea that made these islands a prison makes them extraordinary.

02Culture

Almost everyone here came from somewhere else

The settler islands are a mix that exists nowhere else: Bengali, Tamil and Telugu families who arrived after Partition and after the jail closed, cooking coconut and mustard in the same kitchen, serving fish that was in the water at breakfast. Hindi works everywhere. So does patience — nothing moves faster than the next boat.

The older cultures are not on the itinerary and should not be. Several indigenous peoples still live on these islands, some with almost no contact by their own choice, and Indian law protects that choice. The distinction between the islands you may visit and the ones you may not matters more here than anywhere else in the country.

03Practical guidance

The honest logistics

  • Best monthsNovember to April — calm seas and clear water. Crossings turn rough from June through September.
  • Getting thereFly to Port Blair (IXZ) from Chennai, Kolkata, Delhi or Bengaluru, then a catamaran ferry of about 90 minutes to Havelock (Swaraj Dweep).
  • StayingHavelock for beaches and diving, Neil Island (Shaheed Dweep) for quieter days, one night in Port Blair at each end.
  • Days neededSix days is the honest minimum — two of them are travel. Eight lets you dive properly.
  • Budget feelComfortable from ₹4,000/day; beachfront resorts from ₹10,000/day.
04Local insight

What a Saint will tell you

Book ferries weeks ahead in season — they sell out and there is no plan B. Radhanagar is best in the last hour of light, once the day boats have gone. Neil's Bharatpur beats the crowded Elephant Beach for snorkelling. Carry cash, because card machines fail whenever the network does, and expect mobile data to turn patchy once you leave Port Blair.

05Responsible travel

Being a good guest

The reef here is recovering from bleaching: never stand on coral, skip reef-walking tours, and wear a rash guard instead of pouring sunscreen into the water. Along the Andaman Trunk Road, photographing or interacting with Jarawa people is a criminal offence — no guide should offer it, and none should be asked.
