Gujarat · Western India

Ahmedabad

A walled city that still works

A pol in the walled city

01Story

Six hundred years and still the same lanes

Ahmed Shah founded his capital on the Sabarmati in 1411, and the walled city he laid out is still where the place actually lives. In 2017 UNESCO made it India's first World Heritage City — not for one monument but for the whole dense, inhabited fabric of it.

The second founding was in 1917, when Gandhi set up his ashram on the west bank and ran a national movement from a room containing a spinning wheel and a low writing desk. The Dandi March left from here in 1930. The desk is still there, and it is smaller than you expect.

02Culture

Gated lanes and unlimited lunch

The old city is built as pols — gated residential clusters, each with its own well, its own bird feeder and, often, a passage built for escaping riots. Carved wooden facades lean over lanes barely wide enough for a scooter. The municipal heritage walk leaves from Kalupur most mornings around eight and is the least touristy good thing here. It starts, fittingly, at the Swaminarayan Mandir — built in 1822, the first temple of the Swaminarayan sampradaya, its carved teak courtyard as good as anything on the route.

Food is the other argument for coming. The Gujarati thali is unlimited by design and faintly sweet by habit, and Manek Chowk becomes a street kitchen after ten at night. Gujarat is a dry state — no alcohol — which is less of a loss than you would think.

03Practical guidance

The honest logistics

  • Best monthsNovember to February. From March it gets serious, and May can pass 43°C.
  • Getting thereFly to Ahmedabad (AMD), or take a fast train from Mumbai — roughly six hours on the express services.
  • StayingRestored haveli stays inside the walled city for atmosphere; Ashram Road or SG Highway for modern hotels.
  • Days neededTwo for the old city and the ashram; three lets you add Modhera and Patan as a day trip — Modhera for its eleventh-century Sun Temple, Patan for the Rani ki Vav stepwell and its carved Vishnu avatars.
  • Budget feelComfortable from ₹2,200/day; heritage haveli stays from ₹6,000/day.
04Local insight

What a Saint will tell you

Walk the pols early, while the heat is bearable and the bird feeders are busy. Adalaj stepwell, twenty kilometres north, is several degrees cooler underground than anything above it and largely empty by mid-morning. The Calico Museum of Textiles is extraordinary and needs booking well in advance — most people find that out too late. And say no twice to more thali; the first no is read as politeness.

05Responsible travel

Being a good guest

The pols are homes, not exhibits — the courtyards behind those carved doors belong to families. Ask before stepping in or photographing. Buy block-print and patola from craft collectives rather than hotel lobbies, and carry a refillable bottle: this is a hot city, and the plastic adds up fast.

06Next journey

Where this path continues

Travellers who take to Ahmedabad's carved stone and lived-in lanes usually get on well with Jodhpur — another old walled city, another stepwell at its centre, drier air. Udaipur is the softer option in the same direction.
