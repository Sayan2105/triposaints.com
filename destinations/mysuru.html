Karnataka · South India

Mysuru

A kingdom that kept its manners

Mysore Palace at dusk

01Story

A kingdom that never quite stopped being one

The Wadiyars ruled from here for the better part of six centuries, and the city still carries itself accordingly — wide avenues, planted trees, civic buildings that expect to be looked at. The palace you visit is the fourth on the site. The one before it burned down in 1897, and Henry Irwin rebuilt it in grey granite and pink domes by 1912.

On Sunday evenings and right through Dasara they switch on close to a hundred thousand bulbs and the whole building becomes an outline of itself. Families arrive with children on shoulders. Nobody treats it as a monument — it is simply the thing at the centre of town.

02Culture

Silk, sandalwood and a thousand steps

Mysuru makes things. Silk woven with real gold zari, sandalwood oil, agarbatti that scents entire streets near the market. Devaraja Market has been trading since the 1800s — pyramids of kumkum powder, bananas in half a dozen varieties, women stringing jasmine by the metre and selling it the same way.

This is also where Ashtanga yoga was taught before it went everywhere else; Gokulam still fills each winter with students who stay for months. Climb Chamundi Hill's thousand-odd steps at dawn if you want to meet Mysuru rather than look at it. The Nandi bull two-thirds of the way up is carved from a single rock, and at the top the Chamundeshwari temple holds the goddess who killed the buffalo demon Mahishasura — the fight the city is named for, Mahishuru worn down over the centuries to Mysuru.

03Practical guidance

The honest logistics

  • Best monthsOctober to February — dry and mild. Dasara falls in September or October and books the city out.
  • Getting thereBengaluru (BLR) is the practical airport; the expressway takes about three hours, trains about two.
  • StayingHeritage hotels near the palace for atmosphere; Gokulam is greener and quieter for longer stays.
  • Days neededTwo days for the city; three if you add Srirangapatna and the Ranganathittu birds.
  • Budget feelComfortable from ₹2,000/day; heritage stays from ₹6,000/day.
04Local insight

What a Saint will tell you

Visit the palace on a weekday morning before the coaches, and don't bother with the audio guide — the ceilings do the work. Mysore pak at Guru Sweet Mart on Sayyaji Rao Road is the original, sold by weight and best eaten warm on the pavement. Srirangapatna is forty minutes away and holds Tipu Sultan's summer palace and his grave, which most visitors skip entirely. While you are on the island, step into the Ranganathaswamy temple it is named for — Vishnu reclining on the serpent Adisesha, in worship since the ninth century, predating Tipu by the better part of nine hundred years.

05Responsible travel

Being a good guest

Buy silk and sandalwood from the government emporium or a working weaver rather than a hotel lobby — the mark-up is the least of the problem. Chamundi Hill's steps are a pilgrimage route, not a fitness trail; leave nothing on them. And say no to elephant rides, here and anywhere else in Karnataka.

06Next journey

Where this path continues

People who like Mysuru's stone and lineage usually love Hampi — north across Karnataka, an entire imperial capital left standing in a boulder field. Madurai is the other direction: older, louder, still wholly a temple town.
