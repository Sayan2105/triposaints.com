Himachal Pradesh · The Indian Himalaya

Spiti Valley & Kaza

A cold desert that keeps its own hours

Key monastery above the Spiti river

01Story

A trench between the Himalaya and Tibet

Spiti is a river valley in the rain shadow — between 3,000 and 4,500 metres, almost no rainfall, nothing growing without irrigation, and rock in every colour except green. Barely twelve thousand people live along it. For several months each winter, when the passes shut, they are largely on their own.

Villages appear where the meltwater channels reach, so they arrive as sudden green rectangles in a brown valley. It is the most legible landscape in India: you can see at a glance exactly why every house stands where it does.

02Culture

A thousand years of mud walls

Spiti has been Buddhist for a millennium and its buildings say so. Tabo monastery was founded in 996 CE and its mud-walled halls still carry their original murals — no photography, very little light, and worth the hour it asks of you. Key gompa stacks up its hillside above the river like something remembered rather than planned.

Ordinary life is agricultural and short-seasoned: peas and barley through the summer, then a long shut-in winter. Homestays are how most visitors meet it — a room in a family house, thukpa and butter tea, and a conversation that runs longer than you had planned for.

03Practical guidance

The honest logistics

  • Best monthsMid-June to early October for the full circuit. The Shimla side stays open most of the year; the Manali pass does not.
  • Getting thereTwo roads: Shimla–Kinnaur (two days, gradual, far kinder to your body) or Manali over Kunzum La (one long day, summer only). In by one, out by the other.
  • StayingHomestays in Kaza, Langza, Komic and Kibber. Hotels exist in Kaza; the homestays are better and cheaper.
  • Days neededEight to ten to do the circuit properly. Under six and you are driving, not seeing.
  • Budget feelHomestays from ₹1,200/day with meals; ₹3,000/day covers a comfortable trip.
04Local insight

What a Saint will tell you

Go up via Shimla. The Manali road climbs over 2,500 metres to Kunzum La in a single day, and altitude sickness in Kaza is common and entirely avoidable. Carry cash — the ATMs run dry and cards mostly don't work. Fill fuel in Kaza; the next dependable pump is a long way in either direction. Chandratal earns the detour, but sleep lower down.

05Responsible travel

Being a good guest

Spiti's water comes off glaciers that are visibly retreating, and every bottle you buy stays in the valley afterwards. Bring a filter, take your waste out with you, choose homestays that keep the money in the villages, and ask before photographing anyone — monks included.

06Next journey

Where this path continues

Spiti and Ladakh are the same landscape under different administrations, and people who love one usually want the other. Ladakh is larger, better served, and reachable by air on the day you have had enough of the road.
