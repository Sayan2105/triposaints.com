Karnataka · South India

Bengaluru

The city that keeps rebuilding itself

Malleswaram in the morning

01Story

Four cities stacked on one plateau

Bengaluru sits at about 920 metres, which is why the British put a cantonment here and why the evenings still cool down in a way no other big Indian city manages. It was a garden town, then a science town, then the place the world outsourced its software to — and it never paused to finish any of those versions of itself.

The result is a city of layers rather than monuments. A sixteenth-century market street runs into a glass office park; a temple queue forms outside a brewery. People arrive here to work and stay for the weather.

02Culture

Breakfast is the serious meal

Kannada is the city's language, but Bengaluru has absorbed everyone — Tamil, Telugu, Malayalam, Hindi and several versions of English, often inside one auto ride. Mornings are settled at a darshini counter: masala dosa eaten standing, filter coffee poured between tumbler and davara until it foams.

Evenings belong to whatever you are into — theatre at Ranga Shankara, a book club in Cubbon Park, an independent gig, a taproom that takes its own beer far too seriously. This is the one Indian metro where the default social unit is a small room of people who met over an interest.

Old Bangalore keeps its own calendar. In Basavanagudi, the sixteenth-century Bull Temple — Kempegowda's granite Nandi, four and a half metres of it — gave the neighbourhood its name and still anchors a groundnut fair each winter. And on a full-moon night each spring the Karaga, the city's oldest festival, leaves the Dharmaraya Swamy Temple after midnight and processes barefoot through the old pete until dawn.

03Practical guidance

The honest logistics

  • Best monthsOctober to February — dry and mild. April and May are hotter than the city's reputation suggests.
  • Getting thereKempegowda International (BLR) is 35–40 km north; allow 60–90 minutes into town. Fast trains and buses from Chennai, Mysuru and Hyderabad.
  • StayingIndiranagar or Koramangala for food and bars; Malleswaram for old Bangalore; MG Road for central and forgettable.
  • Days neededTwo days for the city. A third buys you Nandi Hills or a day out to Mysuru.
  • Budget feelComfortable from ₹3,000/day; good hotels and restaurant dinners from ₹7,000/day.
04Local insight

What a Saint will tell you

Plan around traffic, not distance — twelve kilometres can take an hour at six in the evening, and the Metro beats any cab on the routes it covers. The queue at Vidyarthi Bhavan is shortest before eight. Cubbon Park closes to cars on Sundays, which is the only way to see it properly. And Nandi Hills only works if you leave by 4.30 am.

05Responsible travel

Being a good guest

The city's lakes and groundwater are under real strain, and Bengaluru has run short of water within living memory. Refill bottles instead of buying them, and don't treat long showers as a hotel perk. Tip auto drivers and delivery riders properly — they absorb the traffic everyone else complains about.
